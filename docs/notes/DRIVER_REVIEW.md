# Glance review by a simulated racecar driver (and what it means for the dash)

> **Working note, 2026-11-05.** The review below is the judgement of a **subagent role-playing an experienced GT3 driver**, shown renders of the three pages of 1.2 with simulated telemetry. It is an informed opinion, not a test with a real driver, and its figures about the sun come from a model with **assumed** numbers. Paths are relative to the repository root. The decision it leads to (a glance page) is **not built**: it changes what the dash shows and is the owner's call.

## The conditions the review used
The owner's set-up: an 11 inch 16:9 screen (243.5 x 137.0 mm, 1920 x 1080, 0.1268 mm per pixel) 75 cm away, its centre 20 degrees to the right of the eye line, bright sun, full speed, 500 ms to read it. Computed from that (**computed**):

| | |
|---|---|
| 1 px | 0.58 arcmin; 1 degree = 103 px; 1 arcmin = 1.72 px |
| the screen subtends | 18.4 x 10.4 degrees, from 10.8 to 29.2 degrees right of the eye line |
| one fixation | about 206 px across sharply (2 degrees); large type usable out to about 516 px (5 degrees) from the fixation point |
| character height | cap height is about 0.7 x font size. 11 px = 4.5 arcmin, 15 px = 6, 26 px = 10.6, 38 px = 15.5, 52 px = 21 arcmin |
| common glance rule of thumb (from memory, approximate) | at least 16 arcmin (about 39 px font) as a minimum, 20 to 30 arcmin (49 to 74 px) for what must be read in one fixation |

Sun (**model, assumed**: about 25 000 lux on the screen, 3.8% matte reflectance, 300 cd/m2 panel, so the reflected veil is as bright as the screen): contrast against the dash's panel colour falls from 17:1 to **1.9:1** for white and signal yellow, 1.55 for the mint green, 1.4 to 1.5 for cyan, orange and blue, **1.25 for the brake red**, 1.2 and below for the grey labels. The arithmetic is `node tools/viewing_geometry.js` (change the first lines for another screen, distance or sun).

## What the driver said
**Page 1 (Drive).** A workbench, not a dash. In 500 ms it gets the yellow phase block (which tells it what it is already doing), the white gear and speed (barely, glyphs about 17 arcmin), the yellow arrow of the car drawing and a salmon smear where the delta is. The rev bar (120 px) is no shift cue. The rest is gone.
**Page 2 (Tyres and car).** A tyre engineer's page. Four mint temperatures at 38 px (15.5 arcmin) at 1.55:1; nothing says "all fine" or "act now".
**Page 3 (Braking and logs).** A debrief report. The cold-blue word and the red braking-zone line vanish; oil and water temperatures are in 10 px text. Usable in the pits.

**What it wanted in the 500 ms glance on page 1, ranked:** a full-width shift strip (1920 x 40 px, last segments yellow, flashing near the limiter), the gear at about 220 px, the delta to best as a signed bar (800 x 50 px) with the number at about 110 px, speed at about 100 px, position and gap at about 70 px; everything white or yellow, colour only as a second cue next to a shape or a word. Fuel margin and flag only as big alerts. **Missing:** shift lights, a live delta bar, lap n of m, last-lap time flashed for three seconds after the line, the flag as a full-width shape, alert banners (TC/ABS, oil, water, brake temperature), a brake-bias change flash, fuel margin as one signed number. **Remove from the glance:** the six phase tabs, the data-source chip and the footer, track and air temperature, the load in newtons, the steering-wheel picture, body slip as the hero number ("I feel slip, I do not read degrees"), the corner counters. The understeer word next to a grip verdict on a straight is a false alarm and should show per corner only.
**Page 2** wanted a four-corner matrix in car layout with a temperature at about 110 px plus a hot / in-window / cold shape, pressure delta at 55 px ("OK" inside plus or minus 0.5 psi), disc temperatures under each corner, wear at 50 px and laps left at this wear rate; the tread bars, suspension traces, pitch and roll pictures and g traces dropped. **Page 3** wanted brake bias at about 120 px, ABS as an inverted flashing block at 90 px, the last braking zone at 80 px, the lock-up count at 70 px, and the corner verdict as a word at 50 px; oil and water on the glance strip.
**Sun rules:** only white and signal yellow reach 1.9:1, so drop orange, red, blue and greys as text colours; no trace thinner than about 6 px; alerts as filled inverted blocks flashing at about 2 Hz ("motion and area beat contrast in the sun"). A hood over the panel is worth more than any recolour.
**Pages in use:** make the first page the glance page; page order glance, corners (tyres and brakes), debrief (laps, sectors, logs, g, suspension). At full speed it will not hunt for a button, so add a button that always returns to page 1 and show the page as a big pager, not 10 px text. No automatic page change on braking (content moving under a saccade is worse than content that is absent); only two automatic rules, speed under 50 km/h or in the pit lane shows the tyres-and-brakes page, speed over 100 km/h returns to the glance page. An always-on glance strip (the top 120 px of every page: shift bar, gear, delta bar, gap, flag word) would make the buttons matter less.
**"Do not just enlarge the panels."** Scaling by 1.6 takes 15 px text to 24 px (9.8 arcmin), far under the 16 arcmin minimum; reaching 39 px needs about 2.6 times, which about 40 numbers on a page cannot survive.

## What was done with it
The panels were enlarged as far as each page allows (the request that came with the review): page 1 slip view 1.64 times and the other three panels 1.25 times; page 2 tyres 1.7 times, suspension and dynamics 1.18 times; page 3 braking 1.6 times, events 1.58 times (`PAGES` in `build/build_dash.js`; every size, font, line thickness and the formulas that return a size or a coordinate scale with the panel, and `test/paging.test.js` checks the formulas). That makes pages 2 and 3 readable at a glance in good light and page 1 about 1.6 times better, **but it does not reach the sizes above**: the review's point stands.

## Decision for the owner: a glance page
The review's single biggest change is a new page 1 of five big chunks (shift strip, gear, delta bar, speed, gap), with the rest moved to later pages or dropped, and a thin always-on strip on every page. That means new elements (shift bar, delta bar, alert banners, pager, last-lap flash), removing things people may like (phase tabs, the slip number as hero) and one new rule (return to page 1 above 100 km/h). Nothing of it is built. Data check before building: shift light thresholds (rpm and max rpm exist), pit limiter and TC / ABS map level (not yet checked in SimHub's catalog), oil and water are exposed.

## What was built, and the second review (2026-11-05)
The glance page was built as `TelemetryMasterSuite_v1_3` (README, CHANGELOG 1.3.0) and shown to the same simulated driver again with renders in normal light and with the sun veil. It judged page 1 "a dash now" (five chunks in 500 ms: gear, speed, delta number and bar, position and gaps, the shift bar as a peripheral blob) and found the panel pages still documents. Changes made from that second review: the GAP and KM/H labels removed; fuel and lap moved under the delta bar and the fuel margin turned yellow only when the fuel is nearly gone; TC and ABS shrunk and latched; the alert centred, carrying its value, debounced and flashing for 3 s only, with the flag in its own band; a shift bar that closes in from both edges, gets taller from 90% and flashes on and off (white and yellow are the same luminance in sun); the delta scale tightened to half a second; arrows instead of signs for the gaps; the strip on pages 2 to 4 gathered into one cluster with an alert that turns it into the alert word; the pager's inactive squares outlined; the fuel to add below 60 km/h; fuel for two laps or less as an alert; a brake-bias change as a hint.

**Not done, and why.** Automatic page changes by speed and a jump-to-page-1 button are not possible in SimHub (the screen enable expression is only evaluated when you switch screens, and only next and previous exist). A pit-lane overlay needs a pit property that was not checked. **Needs your decision** (changes what the dash shows): cut to three pages (Glance, Corners with the tyres and discs per corner, Debrief); simplify the tyre cards to a temperature at about 110 px with a hot / in-window / cold shape plus the pressure delta and move the discs next to them; make the discs the braking page (2 x 2 at 100 px) and brake bias at 120 px; delete the driving-phase tab row on page 4 (the driver finds it duplicates the strip); raise the small text on page 4 (the right column is still 11 to 14 px).

## The owner's answer: it is a side panel (2026-11-05)
After driving 1.3 the owner said the dash is a side panel for detailed telemetry beside a main dash with several pages (Lovely Dashboard XL, which I read: speed, gear, pit limiter, shift LEDs, lap times, sectors, relatives, standings, tyres and wheels, fuel, flags, session, damage, TC / ABS / brake-bias settings; nothing on slip, balance or lock-ups), that the lock-up pop-up in the middle is the best part, and that they leave the dash on the slip and balance page because it holds the data on oversteer and understeer. So the glance page and the top strip repeat the main dash. 1.4 (`TelemetryMasterSuite_v1_4`) therefore leads with the slip and balance page (and, at the owner's request, has a slide pop-up like the lock-up one), drops the strip, shows the alert pop-up on every page and keeps the glance page last. The glance review above still applies to the pop-up and to the balance data itself.

After the owner drove 1.4 (no flag pop-ups, small suspension graphs and logs, a useless page 4, small load bars and inputs) a driver subagent was asked again, this time knowing the owner has an in-game dash and an external DDU in front of them, then a race engineer and a graphic designer. The result is three pages and two pop-up lanes; see `DESIGN_1_4.md`.

# Plan: a paged and slimmer dash (1.2)

> **Status (2026-11-05).** The owner chose three pages and 1.2 was built as `TelemetryMasterSuite_v1_2`: the three pages (Drive; Tyres and car; Braking and logs), nothing cut (the L2 slimming list below is **not** part of it), and the interval offsets and cheaper hot formulas of L0 not done. What changed on the way: the counters that were private to a formula (sector timer, fuel per lap, braking totals, disc peaks) had to move into the pump, found by `test/paging.test.js`. Measured afterwards on an idle machine: a page costs 2.6 to 4.7 ms a pass and 31 to 61 ms a second (model), against 13.2 ms and 101 ms a second for all of 1.1; the milliseconds in the tables below came from a busy machine and are about 45% too high (their shares hold). See `PERFORMANCE.md`. The panels were later enlarged to fill their pages and a simulated driver reviewed the result (`DRIVER_REVIEW.md`). The rest of this note is the plan as it was written, before any 1.2 code, and was not rewritten.

> **Planning note.** Written after 1.1, before any 1.2 code. Nothing here is built or has been seen running. Every claim is labelled **measured**, **read from code**, **inferred** or **guess**. Paths are relative to the repository root. Background and the numbers behind 1.1 are in [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md).

What this says:
- The slip view is the biggest per-second cost (29%) because its formulas are the fast ones (pump at frame rate, load-centre dot at 33 ms), not because it has the most formulas.
- The worst single frame (everything due at once) is still about 24 ms. Whether bursts like that, or the 19% average, is what the owner feels is **not known**.
- The cause of the lag is **still unproven** (PERFORMANCE.md). 1.1 may already be enough; this plan should not be started before the owner's report on 1.1 (the `PERF` line, smoother or not, dash stutter or game frame rate).

## What the experiments settled (2026-11-04)
Run on the real SimHub 9.12.9 with **no game running** (so it ticks at its idle rate, 10 passes a second), through its own web client in a headless Edge. The test dash is generated by `tools/page_probe.js` (five screens, formulas that count how often they run) and driven by `tools/page_check.js`. The overlay on the second monitor is the same client, so the same rules apply (**inferred**).

| Question | Result (measured) |
|---|---|
| Does the client's NextScreen / PreviousScreen action switch screens? | Yes: A, B, A, then back to B. A screen whose enable expression returns false was skipped. |
| Are the formulas of the screen that is not shown evaluated? | **No.** On B the counters of A read 0.0 a second; on A the counter of B read 0.0. The shown screen ran at the idle rate (10 a second). |
| Are the formulas of children of a layer whose `Visible` is bound to false evaluated? | **No** (0.0 a second), while the layer's own `Visible` formula keeps running (10 a second). A static `Visible = false` item: 0.0, as before. |
| Is a screen's enable expression evaluated all the time? | No: 0.0 a second while idle. It looks to be evaluated when the screen is switched (**inferred**), so it cannot drive automatic paging. |
| Does a foreground-layer or background-layer screen show up with every screen? | It did not appear in this test (flags: in-game screen and foreground layer). Unexplained; none of the five examples uses one. Plan: copy the header onto every page. |
| Does SimHub's NCalc have what a plain binding needs? | Yes: `if()`, `Round()` and lower-case `round()`, `format()`, `?:`, `isnull()`, `Floor()` and `Max()` all gave the expected text. |

What follows for the plan: paging really removes formula work (no "inactive screens are still evaluated" risk), the switch needs no plugin, and the plain-binding part of L0 is possible. The real wheel button on the real overlay: the owner tried it with the test dash and reported that it works (2026-11-05); the two reachable screens flipped.

## Three levers, cheapest first
**L0. Free changes (nothing visible changes).** *Inferred, small:* offset the refresh intervals a little (100, 110, 120 ... instead of all 100) so formulas do not all fall due in one frame; cheaper versions of the hottest formulas (the load-centre dot costs 150 us each for Left and Top, the phase-tab colour 364 us); plain `[Property]` bindings where a formula only reads one property (the examples do this for pedals and gears); a burst counter on the `PERF` line (most formulas run in one frame) so the effect shows in the game. Needs checking in SimHub's formula editor: that the plain binding gives the identical text and colour (units, rounding).

**L1. Pages.** Panels that are not on the page being shown cost nothing (**measured**: a hidden item's formulas do not run), and are not drawn.
- *What makes it safe:* the detectors run in `tms_pump`, carried by one formula (`betaRot`, slip view). If that panel is off screen the detectors stop and the corner and event logs miss corners (**read from code**, `dash/js/tms_dash.js`). In 1.2 every page carries a copy of the header strip and the pump carrier. (A foreground-layer screen, which would hold them once, did not show in my test and none of the examples uses one, so I would not rely on it.)
- *Two pages* (Drive: header, slip, inputs, lap timing, fuel; Analyse: tyres, braking, dynamics, suspension, logs): worst page about 100 and about 117 ms/s (**model**, the pump and header stay on both), items per page about 310 and 400, charts 7 and 21. *Three pages* (Drive; Tyres, suspension, dynamics; Braking and logs): worst page about 100, charts 7, 15 and 6. The examples show about 3 charts per page and 9 at most, so 21 charts on one page is the weak spot.
- *Consequences the owner should know:* a chart on a page that is not shown receives no points (the client drops them while the chart is hidden and the server does not evaluate it), so after a switch it shows its old trace and carries on from there, with a jump in time where the page was away (**read from code**, `renderChartItem` in the served `controls.js`; not run). Auto-range buffers kept in `root` are stale for a moment after a switch. A panel is off screen part of the time, which cuts against the dense see-everything design.
- *The switch:* each page is a separate **Screen** of the dash, switched by SimHub's own actions "Show next dash screen" and "Show previous dash screen" (names found in `SimHubWPF.exe`; they appear in Controls and events, so a wheel button or a rotary can be mapped to them). The web client calls the same server action from its on-screen arrows and from the left / right arrow keys (**read from code**, `cc.js` of the served client). The experiment below ran that action: it switches and the pages cycle. **Not tested:** a real wheel button moving the overlay on the second monitor (needs the owner, 3 minutes with the test dash described below). A second route, panels in a layer whose `Visible` is bound, needs something to hold the page number, and I know of none; the examples take it from properties of their own plugin, which we do not have.
- *Layout:* the dash is generated from an HTML mock with the panels placed on one 1920x1080 grid (`build/geometry.json`). The cheap way is to keep the grid and leave a dark hole where a panel is off the page. The proper way gives each page its own arrangement (bigger panels), which means editing the mock. I have not sized that.

**L2. Slimming (changes what the dash shows; the owner decides each item).** From the slim position paper (measured against 1.0.0, not yet re-measured against 1.1; one multi-line text block per list instead of one formula per row, plain bindings, fixed ranges instead of auto-range):

| Cut | What is lost |
|---|---|
| Phase tabs become a moving bar | the lit tab fill |
| Balance meter: 9 segments become one needle | the segment look |
| Sector tiles in one text | purple best-sector highlight, per-tile best |
| Delta chart and tyre core chart fixed range | autoscale |
| Lap table, event log, corner log as colour channels | the exact per-row colours (corner log keeps amber / red / green) |
| Tyre pressure traces (4 charts) | the traces |
| Grip card extras, band word, last-corner card, load-centre dot | those items |
| Braking: word, rate, PAD/DISC, ABS word, overlap and ABS mini-charts | those items |
| Raw-steering debug text, oil / water line, `raceText`, `lapSub` | those items |

Their author measured 62.8 to 13.4 ms per pass against 1.0.0 (and 77 ms/s against 2156 under the interval model). The equivalent gain on top of 1.1 is not measured; I would expect a smaller one, because 1.1 already removed the duplicated detectors (**guess**).

## Order of work, each step ends with a decision or a check
1. **Gate (owner, 2 minutes).** The report on 1.1. If the game's frame rate does not change when the dash is closed, stop here: more formula work will not help.
2. **Experiments (me, no owner needed, headless render of SimHub's own web client). Done, see above.** Inactive screens and hidden layers are not evaluated, a headless render can switch screens, and NCalc has `if()`, `Round()` and `format()`. Still to check per binding while building: that a plain binding gives the identical text and colour as the formula it replaces.
2b. **Wheel check. Done (reported by the owner, 2026-11-05).** With `TMSPageProbe` selected and a wheel button mapped to "Show next dash screen", the pages flip. The probe has five screens but only two can be reached (the disabled one is skipped and the foreground / background layers do not show), so it showed two pages; a real dash can have as many in-game screens as it needs, since the action cycles through all enabled ones.
3. **L0 as 1.2.** New package `TelemetryMasterSuite_v1_2` beside 1.0.0 and 1.1; identity test against the frozen 1.0.0 library keeps the output identical.
4. **L1 pages** (only if the owner can switch pages from the wheel or a key, and agrees the split). Tests: every item sits on exactly one page or on all pages; the pump carrier is on every page; the detectors give the same output with one page's formulas evaluated and the others not.
5. **L2 cuts**, only the ones the owner picks, one at a time, each with its before and after screenshot from the real client.

## What would make this plan wrong
- Formula evaluation is not the lag. Then pages help only through fewer drawn items and charts, and L2 not at all.
- With more than two in-game screens the action cycles through them in order; I have only seen two reachable screens in a test, so a three-page dash needs the same quick wheel check once built (**inferred** that it cycles A, B, C).
- The 33 ms formulas do not run at 30 Hz in a race. The `PERF` line shows the real rates.
- The model counts only formula time. SimHub's own work per frame (property reads, serialising, sending) is not in it.
